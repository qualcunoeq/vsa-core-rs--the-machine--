## What counts as commercial use

The license treats commercial use broadly. It includes use for the benefit of a for-profit entity, internal company research and development even when nothing is sold, trading and investment activity, revenue-generating products and services, hosted or API services, consulting, and any direct or indirect commercial advantage. The full definition is in the Commercial Use section of the [license](LICENSE).

This is deliberate. A use does not become noncommercial merely because it is internal, is labeled research, or is not sold on its own.

## Source availability

Permitted noncommercial use is not a loophole for source closure. If you distribute a modified version, or make a modified version available to users over a network, you must provide the Corresponding Source under the same license, identify your changes, and preserve the copyright and attribution notices. The details are in the Source Availability section of the [license](LICENSE).

## Contributions and commercial rights

To keep commercial licensing possible for the whole project, contributions are accepted only under [CLA.md](CLA.md). The contributor keeps ownership of the contribution and grants the author the rights needed to distribute it under the research license and under commercial licenses. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Source-available, not open source

Because the license does not permit use for any purpose, including commercial purposes, it is not an open source license under the Open Source Initiative definition. Describe The Machine as source-available or research-source, not as open source.
